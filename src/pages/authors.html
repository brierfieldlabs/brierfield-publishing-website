Distinct voices belong on the page.

Author

Ben Blackwood

Ben Blackwood writes atmospheric British horror set in places that ought to feel safe. His stories begin in the recognisable world of families, familiar routines and ordinary decisions, then follow the consequences when something impossible breaks through. He is interested in the human cost of fear, the secrets people protect and the uneasy distance between what can be explained and what can only be survived.

His work favours believable characters, slow-building dread and horror that leaves a mark. His novels are in development, and no debut title or publication date has yet been announced.

Author

Fraser Verran

Fraser Verran writes traditional British murder mysteries with dry humour and a particular interest in the consequences of a badly worded agenda. His Nether Fell stories unfold among the villages and hills of a fictional Cumbrian district, where the crimes are serious, the clues are fair and no disagreement is too small to survive for thirty years.

When he isn't writing, Fraser enjoys walking in weather most people would avoid, collecting notebooks he may never fill and reading village noticeboards with unwarranted attention. He lives somewhere in the North of England and has asked his publisher not to be more specific.

Illustrator

Cyril Tinsley

Cyril Tinsley has been friends with Fraser Verran since primary school, and every Nether Fell mystery includes several of his black-ink illustrations. Cyril lives in the district and has spent years drawing village-hall notices, concert programmes and explanatory diagrams, including several nobody remembers requesting. He believes a good illustration should be clear, economical and useful, though not everybody agrees on which of those qualities matters most.

Cyril has a particular eye for stonework, knitting and the small print on official notices. His work is carefully observed, if not always strictly proportioned, and he maintains that perspective is best approached with confidence. Brierfield Publishing is pleased to let him get on with it.
